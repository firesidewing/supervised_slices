# Product

## Register

both

The primary surface context determines which register applies per task. `/kitchen/*` and `/menu/*` routes are **product**. `/`, `/sign-in`, `/register`, and marketing pages are **brand**. When no surface is specified, default to **product**.

## Users

Independent pizza shop owners and operators, typically 1-3 location businesses. They are skilled at running a kitchen but often frustrated by the cost and friction of digital tools that weren't built for them. They use Chitline during active service (high stress, no time to think) and during off-hours (planning menus, reviewing orders). The kitchen view is glanced at under pressure; the menu editor is used with more patience.

## Product Purpose

Chitline is an all-in-one SaaS platform for independent pizza shops: zero-commission online ordering, kitchen queue management, and menu editing in one place. It replaces Slice, DoorDash, and paper tickets. Success looks like an operator who handles more orders with fewer mistakes and keeps every dollar of margin they earn.

The three core modules:
- **The Rail**: live kitchen queue, order management
- **The Studio**: menu editor
- **The Counter**: customer-facing ordering

## Brand Personality

Direct, Fast, Honest.

The brand speaks plainly, respects that the user is running a business, and never oversells. No buzzwords, no startup-speak. Confidence comes from competence, not from aesthetics. The tool earns trust by working.

## Anti-references

- **DoorDash / Grubhub**: consumer delivery app aesthetics, bold reds, promotional loudness, marketplace feel. We are a tool for operators, not a consumer discovery platform.
- **Generic SaaS (HubSpot, Salesforce)**: enterprise visual language, navy, sterile, heavy form density. Chitline is built for a trade, not a corporate sales team.
- **Retro pizza kitsch**: checkered tablecloths, pizza clip-art, "fun" restaurant theming. The aesthetic is artisan and serious, not nostalgic.
- **Toast / Square (POS)**: point-of-sale terminal aesthetics, functional but cold and utilitarian. Chitline should feel designed, not installed.

## Design Principles

1. **Respect the operator's time.** Every screen surfaces what matters in the next 30 seconds. No decorative chrome, no onboarding modals during service, no clicks to find the obvious.
2. **Honesty as aesthetic.** The zero-commission promise is the brand's core. That directness should show up in copy, pricing displays, and UI: no hidden states, no dark patterns, no ambiguous labels.
3. **Craft-forward, tool-first.** The editorial Atelier aesthetic elevates the product above commodity POS tools. But craft never slows the work. If an animation or layout choice adds friction during service, it loses.
4. **Earned confidence.** Premium feel comes from precision (spacing, type contrast, tonal layering), not from decoration. The product looks expensive because it works, not because it has a gradient.
5. **Built for the trade.** This is professional software for people who take their work seriously. The UI should match that register: dense when the job demands it, spacious when there's room to breathe.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Kitchen environments have variable lighting and operators may be glancing at screens while working. Ensure sufficient contrast on all interactive states and loading indicators. Reduced-motion support should be available; no motion should be required for task completion.
